## 3.2 Identity: who is this community for?

**The "ideal member" persona - describe in 3–5 sentences:**

They're a hands-on technologist somewhere on the spectrum from curious working engineer to ambitious student. The engineer might design or verify hardware, write firmware, or work in an adjacent systems field in the Kanata/Ottawa ecosystem, and they're drawn to RISC-V because it's the open standard their day job rarely gives them room to explore. The student is in an EE/CS program (or recently out of one), looking to go deeper than coursework and build a real network and portfolio in open hardware. What unites them is temperament more than title: they'd rather build, break, and contribute than sit through marketing pitches, they're generous with what they know, and on a Saturday afternoon you might find them soldering, poking at an FPGA, reading an ISA spec, or filing their first PR against an open-source core. They show up to learn in public and leave having helped someone else do the same.

**The "this isn't for you" boundary:**

This isn't a vendor pitching venue or a recruiting channel. Sponsors and companies are definitely welcome as participants, but the meetup floor is not a sales booth or a captive talent pipeline, and visibility never buys editorial control or vendor cheerleading. It's also not the place for buzzword speculation that name-drops RISC-V without real technical engagement. And while everyone is welcome to start as a listener, this is a participatory community, not a lecture series; the people who get the most out of it are the ones who eventually contribute something back.

## 3.3 Success: what does success look like for the community?

*Not for the org, but for the community as a living entity.*

**In 12 months we'll know we're succeeding if:**

1. We've held 8+ meetups and 3+ workshops
2. We have 20-30 people who'd identify as "active members" if asked (the committed core, distinct from the wider ~75 members/RSVP pool tracked in [§2.9](02-mission-model-canvas.md#29-mission-achievement--impact-factors)), drawn from at least 3 universities/colleges and 5+ employers/organizations
3. We have a steering group of at least 4-8 distinct people sharing responsibility, with no one person carrying everything
4. We have 1-2 sponsors
5. Optional: we incorporate under ONCA and have a bank account
6. Optional: we have submitted a grant application (and ideally successfully received it)

**In 36 months we'll know we're succeeding if:**

1. We've produced and open-licensed at least one substantial curriculum unit (e.g., a 6-week RISC-V intro course) that other groups can adopt.
2. A few community members have taken career moves (job, internship, grad school) where they credit the community.
3. Optional: we've achieved CRA charitable status under "advancement of education."
4. The community is structurally robust to losing any single founder.

**Leading indicators we'll watch monthly:**

* New first-time meetup attendees per month
* Repeat attendance rate
* Active participation in our Discord and on LinkedIn

## 3.4 Values (3–7 max)

*What are our shared values? These should be testable; if a behaviour violates a value, the value should help adjudicate.*

**Open ISA, open community**
- In practice: Default to public meetings, open-licensed materials, transparent finances
- When violated: Private decisions about public matters; closed-door member selection

**Hands-on**
- In practice: Workshops produce things; talks come with code or RTL; "build, break, contribute"
- When violated: All-theory presentations, vendor pitches with no demos

**Vendor-neutral**
- In practice: No exclusive sponsor content; no single-vendor cheerleading; sponsors get visibility, not editorial control
- When violated: Workshop titles that read like product launches; speakers who only know their own stuff and won't compare

**Be excellent to each other**
- In practice: Default to charitable interpretation; address harm directly and quickly
- When violated: Snarky culture; "well actually" gatekeeping; harassment unaddressed

**Respect everyone's time**
- In practice: Events start on time, end on time, have a clear point; async-first comms; no pointless meetings
- When violated: Late-running meetups; meetings without agendas; "quick question" Discord pings that consume hours

**Required baseline (non-negotiable, adopt these):**

- **Be excellent to each other** (The foundational rule from Noisebridge, adopted by every successful hackerspace)
- **Vendor-neutral** (Critical for charitable status and for sustaining trust across the ecosystem)
- **Open ISA, open community** (Decisions, finances, code, curriculum open by default.)
